# Disability Intersection: Code of Ethics

**Status:** DRAFT for Lara Minges's review and approval. Last updated October 18, 2026.

These five commitments guide everything we publish and offer: the website, blog posts, assessments, talks, social media, and services.

## 1. Everything is research-backed

- Every fact, statistic, or health, legal, or program claim has a **citation** to a reliable source (a government agency, peer-reviewed research, a recognized professional or standards body, or the original organization).
- Statistics must link to a **primary source** we have actually opened and read. If we can't verify a number, we don't use it.
- We note the **date** a source was checked, because rules and programs change.
- Our own experience and opinions are welcome, and we label them as such ("In my experience..."). We never present opinion as research.

## 2. We don't put false information out there

- If we aren't sure, we say so, or we leave it out.
- We never invent quotes, sources, testimonials, results, or credentials.
- We only name partners, features, and clients with their permission and only describe what is true.
- If we get something wrong, we **correct it promptly** and note the correction.
- Anything health-related or legal-adjacent is **reviewed before publishing**, and we are clear about what our services are and are not (educational, non-medical, not legal advice).

## 3. We create an encouraging, supportive environment for everyone

- Our tone is warm, respectful, and hopeful. We meet people where they are.
- No shaming of people, bodies, choices, or circumstances.
- We center people's own goals and strengths, and we respect their right to make their own decisions.

## 4. We don't use fear as a selling point

- No scare tactics, false urgency, or "before it's too late" marketing.
- We never exaggerate risks, and we never imply someone is failing or in danger to sell something.
- We describe what we offer honestly and let people decide if it is right for them.

## 5. We are as inclusive as possible

- **Language:** We use person-first language ("people with disabilities") by default, and we follow each person's own preference when we know it.
- **Access:** Our website, documents, talks, and materials are designed to be accessible, with alt text, captions, plain language, and readable design. We welcome feedback and fix barriers.
- **Representation:** We show a wide range of people and experiences, and avoid reducing disability to a single image or story.
- **Privacy and consent:** We share people's stories and images only with their clear permission.

## How we hold ourselves to this

1. Every public post, page, or assessment gets **Lara's approval** before it goes live.
2. Health- and legal-adjacent content gets an additional review, ideally by a qualified professional.
3. We keep a **source list** and a "last reviewed" date on informational content.
4. Anyone can tell us about an error or an access barrier at disabilityintersection@startmail.com, and we will respond.
