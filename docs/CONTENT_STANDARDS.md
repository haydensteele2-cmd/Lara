# Disability Intersection: Content Standards

**Status:** DRAFT. Follows the Code of Ethics (see CODE_OF_ETHICS.md). Last updated October 18, 2026.

## Voice
- Posts are written in **Lara's first-person voice** ("I", "my clients"), and **Lara approves every post** before it is published.
- Personal stories, including living with POTS, appear as Lara chooses, and clients are only identified with permission.
- Warm, plain language and short sentences, with any technical term explained.

## Writing style: lead with the positive
Say what we are and what we do. Describe the strengths, goals, and possibilities in front of us.
- Write "We are fact-driven and supportive" and skip the "not this" half of the sentence.
- Replace "X, not Y" and "instead of Y" sentences with a direct statement of X.
- Describe people by their strengths and goals, and describe services by what they offer.
- Keep necessary safety and legal notes in plain, friendly language (for example, "This works alongside your doctor's care").
- Sound like Lara: use her real words, concrete details, and varied sentence length. Skip stock phrases and formulaic lists of three buzzwords.
- Read each draft aloud. If a sentence sounds like a brochure, rewrite it the way Lara would say it.

## Language
- Default to **person-first language**: "people with disabilities," "children with disabilities," "a person with a disability."
- Follow an individual's own preference when we know it, and quote people in their own words.
- "Disability community" and "disability-led organizations" are fine as community terms.
- Use respectful, current language that treats people as the experts on their own lives (for example, "living with POTS").

## Citations (required)
- **Every fact, statistic, program rule, and health or legal claim gets a numbered citation** at the end of the post with the title, publisher, link, and the date we checked it.
- Prefer primary and official sources: government agencies, peer-reviewed research, and recognized standards bodies.
- Cite only statistics we have opened and read. When we can't find a source, we reword the point as Lara's experience or leave it out.
- Mark secondary sources (blogs, vendor sites) as such, and use them for definitions or context.
- Rules and programs change, so posts with factual content show a "last reviewed" date and are re-checked at least twice a year.

## Health and legal topics
- Every health-related post notes that it is educational and non-medical and works alongside care from a licensed professional.
- Every legal-adjacent post notes that it is general information, and points readers to an attorney or advocate for legal advice.
- Describe services as educational and supportive, and share outcomes when they are documented and cited.
- Extra review before publishing: Lara, plus a qualified professional where possible.

## Structure (SEO and AI answers)
1. Question-style title.
2. A direct answer in the first 40 to 60 words.
3. Short sections with clear headings, and lists where helpful.
4. 3 FAQs that match the page's FAQ markup.
5. Links to the relevant service page.
6. Sources list with dates.

## Publishing checklist
- [ ] Facts checked and cited with links and dates
- [ ] Positive, encouraging framing throughout
- [ ] "People with disabilities" language used
- [ ] Health and legal notes added where needed
- [ ] Images have alt text; contrast and reading level checked
- [ ] Read aloud: sounds like Lara
- [ ] Lara has read and approved the final version
- [ ] Title, description, and byline are set
