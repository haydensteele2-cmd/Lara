Functional Nutrition for Disability & Chronic Illness

A non-medical, food-first approach for kids and adults with disabilities and chronic illness, focused on energy and strength.

By Lara Minges · Updated October 14, 2026

Book a consult See all services

Functional nutrition at Disability Intersection is a non-medical approach to feeling better through food. Lara works with children and adults who have disabilities or chronic illness, starting with a thorough assessment of what's actually going on: what's happening in your body, what's getting in the way, and what we can change.

The goal is energy, strength, and being a happier, more capable version of yourself. Whether you're managing a chronic condition or simply want more in the tank for daily life, the work focuses on what food can do, leading with real, everyday eating before reaching for supplementation.

Good questions

Frequently asked

Quick, clear answers about how this work actually goes.

What does a functional nutritionist do?

They assess what's happening in your body and lead with food to help you feel stronger and more energetic. It's non-medical and wellbeing-focused.

Is this a weight-loss program?

No. The focus is energy, strength, and feeling well, especially for people with disabilities or chronic illness.

How is this different from diet culture?

Diet culture is built around shrinking bodies. This work is built around supporting them: more energy, more strength, and a kinder relationship with food and your body, especially for people with disabilities or chronic illness.

Can this help with chronic illness like POTS?

Yes. Lara has POTS herself and supports clients in doing as much as possible through food for chronic conditions.

This page is educational and non-medical. It works alongside, and does not replace, advice from your doctor or another licensed health professional.
